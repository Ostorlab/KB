Upgrade every PAN-OS and Panorama appliance to a fixed build for its branch, following the per-branch solution table in the vendor advisory. There are no known workarounds — patching is the fix.

* **PAN-OS 12.2** → 12.2.4 or later. **PAN-OS 12.1** → 12.1.4-h12, 12.1.7-h8 or 12.1.10-h2 or later. **PAN-OS 11.2** → 11.2.4-h22, 11.2.7-h21, 11.2.10-h15, 11.2.13-h3 or 11.2.16 or later. **PAN-OS 11.1** → 11.1.4-h37, 11.1.6-h39, 11.1.7-h11, 11.1.10-h35, 11.1.13-h15 or 11.1.16-h3 or later. **PAN-OS 10.2** → 10.2.7-h38, 10.2.10-h41, 10.2.13-h26, 10.2.16-h11 or 10.2.18-h12 or later. Appliances on older unsupported PAN-OS releases should upgrade to a supported fixed version.
* **Restrict access to the management interface** to trusted internal IP addresses or a dedicated jump box, per Palo Alto Networks' best-practice deployment guidance. The vendor's urgency rating for this issue is HIGHEST exactly when the management interface is reachable from external IP addresses; restricting access reduces the risk of exploitation but does not replace the upgrade.
* **Enable the limited Threat Prevention coverage** while upgrades are in flight: Threat IDs 510046 and 510047 (Applications and Threats content 9159-10287 or later), and route management traffic through a DP port, replace the inbound-traffic management certificate, decrypt inbound traffic to the management interface and enable Threat Prevention on it. The vendor is explicit that this coverage is limited — variations of the attack may not be detected — so it complements, not substitutes for, the upgrade.
* **Monitor and respond**: alert on management-interface traffic from the advisory's indicator addresses (`66[.]42[.]72[.]14`, `208[.]83[.]239[.]114`, `149[.]248[.]47[.]234`), review administrative sessions and configuration changes on appliances that were unpatched while exposed, and rotate administrative credentials on any appliance with unexplained activity — the flaw gives an attacker root execution on the device.
